Technical Debt Audit for Startups: Know Your Real Risks

Two weeks. Fixed scope. One deliverable: a complete risk map and a prioritized roadmap to fix it.

Why You Need This

You've built something that works. But lately, every new feature takes longer. Deployments are stressful. Your developers complain about the codebase. You're losing people to burnout.

This is technical debt: shortcuts taken to ship fast, now costing you in maintenance, debugging, and velocity.

The problem? You don't know:

  • Which parts are actually risky vs. which are fine
  • What will break first as you scale
  • How much this is costing you in lost engineering time
  • Where to focus first

So you guess. You reorganize the team. You hire more engineers. Nothing works because you're treating a symptom, not the root cause.

What You Get: A Technical Audit

Two weeks. I dig into your system and deliver:

Risk Map (10-20 pages)

  • Architecture strengths: What's well-designed and scalable
  • Technical debt hotspots: Where the real risks are (security, performance, maintainability)
  • Security findings: Auth flows, data handling, exposure to attacks
  • Scalability assessment: Can this handle 10x traffic without breaking
  • Dependency audit: Outdated libraries, unmaintained packages, known vulnerabilities
  • Test coverage analysis: What's tested, what's not, where failures will hurt

Prioritized Roadmap

Not a wishlist. A 12-month plan ranked by:

  • Business impact (what slows down revenue)
  • Risk (what could break in production)
  • Engineering cost (what's cheap to fix vs. expensive)

Walkthrough & Q&A

We review the findings together. You ask questions. I clarify recommendations and help you understand the tradeoffs.

Real Example: What an Audit Looks Like

A startup with a Laravel + React app, $2M ARR:

What we found:

  • Database queries without indexes on high-traffic endpoints (costing 500ms per request)
  • No rate limiting = vulnerable to scraping and abuse
  • Firebase auth mixed with custom JWT tokens (security risk)
  • Tests exist but only cover happy paths (actual crash bugs hit production)
  • Docker images built without security scanning

What we recommended (prioritized):

  1. Month 1 (Quick wins, 4 weeks): Add rate limiting, fix query performance, unify auth
  2. Month 2-3 (Medium effort, 6 weeks): Expand test coverage on edge cases
  3. Month 4 (Automation, 2 weeks): Docker security scanning in CI
  4. Quarter 2+ (Longer term): Refactor monolith, prepare for microservices

The impact: They fixed the quick wins first. Deployment confidence went up. Their next contractor didn't quit. They're shipping 30% faster.

FAQ

How do I know this is legit and not just a list of everything I could improve?

The roadmap is ranked by business impact and cost. Fixing everything is not the recommendation. I'll tell you what to ignore and what actually matters.

What if I don't like the findings?

We talk about it. If you disagree on something specific, I show you the code. But if your system has real risks, I'm going to tell you honestly.

What if the audit says I need to rewrite everything?

I've never recommended that. Even bad codebases have 20-30% worth keeping. If rewriting is the answer, it's because the cost of fixing is higher than the cost of rewriting - that's rare.

Can I use the audit to decide whether to hire a CTO or contractor?

Yes. Exactly. It's clear documentation of what you actually have, what needs fixing, and what it'll cost. You make a better hiring decision.

How long does the audit take to act on?

Depends on your team. Small teams might do it in 3 months, larger teams in 6-12. But having the roadmap means you're no longer guessing about what to prioritize.
